How to read this · what the columns mean
Record is settled bets won–lost on the US exchanges, flat $100 a bet at the price available before the start. Won v priced is wins against the wins the prices implied — beating that is the whole test, and the ranking sorts on it per bet rather than on ROI, which mostly reflects the prices a pair happened to be offered. Verdict is one ladder on every page. Under 10 settled a pair is Too early and is not ranked at all. From 10 to 30 it is Collecting n/30, with its lean so far: Promising or Behind. At 30+ it is read: Proven is ahead of the prices by z ≥ 2, Working is ahead, No edge is not. If faded is what backing the OTHER side of the same bets would have returned — not this row's ROI with the sign flipped, since both sides of a book are sold above fair, so fading an average rule loses that overround plus its fee; a large positive there means the rule is picking the wrong side, which is a different complaint from having no edge. v the close is how far the price moved toward the pick between the bet and the start, in cents, with t — the mean over its own standard error. It answers a different question and answers it sooner: a win record carries the outcome's own noise, so near an even price a real 2% edge needs thousands of settled bets to reach z 2, while the same closing prices read in tens. Called ahead or behind at t 2 over 20+ fresh closes; level is a real answer, not a missing one. Beating the close is not the same as making money, and nothing is promoted or retired on it alone. Production is entered by hand.

How a lane is replayedentries, exits, fees, control

A lane buys before the event starts, at a set time before it (1 hour before, and a 24-hour variant), at the ask on the first tight quote (spread 3c or less), and at the same moment rests a limit sell at +20%, +30% or +50% ROI after fees. The start comes from the lane's own kickoff source where it has one, otherwise from the price path.

Exits, all from the same entry: the resting limit sell at +20%, +30% or +50%, or none; each with no stop or a −50% stop; otherwise held to Kalshi's settlement. A sell fills only when the bid trades 1c through the limit inside a minute; a minute touching both the stop and the limit takes the stop. Fees: taker 0.07·p(1−p) on entry and on a stop, maker 0.0175·p(1−p) on a limit exit.

The control is the same entry held to settlement, so every lane shows hold against each target (a stop variant against hold with the same stop). A variant is flagged for review at 10 trades; promotion needs 30 AND a better ROI than its hold control. z is clustered by night, never below the independent-trades z. A cell of the form k of n is quotes matched out of the board rows that lane examined: the right series, before its own band. 0 of 0 means nothing was offered. 0 of n means rows were offered and none qualified. A bare number is a count from a run that stored no denominator.
Method
Tipsters and rules name a side and are backed every time; models, books and exchanges state a probability and are backed only on a 3pp disagreement with the price. Polymarket US is the venue wherever it lists a contest; Kalshi is the venue for soccer and anything Polymarket US is missing, with soccer kickoffs taken from ESPN. A contest is logged only with a real book (spread ≤ 5¢), at the ask. Until 2026-09-13 the venue was polymarket.com; those bets still settle there but are not counted when a pair is judged. Quotes logged before the book rule on boxing, cricket and table tennis were voided (pre-gate: logged at an unverified Polymarket placeholder price). Pinnacle prices come through The Odds API: 1 of 1 allowed paid calls last run, 133 credits left until they reset on the 1st. A closing price counts only when taken within 60 minutes of the start (385 of 454 on the pairs still running). A positive ROI under 30 settled bets is not a finding.
