Every source here is logged before the contest starts and stamped with the price that existed at that moment, then settled for real when the market resolves. Two kinds of source are tracked and they are staked differently. Tipsters name a side: that side is backed at the going price, every time, because that is how a tipster is actually followed — high turnover, no Brier score, and a real ROI. Models, books and exchanges state a probability: they are backed only when they disagree with the price by 3pp or more, and they also get an accuracy score. Won v priced is the honest column while samples are small: each backed price is the market's own chance that the bet lands, so their sum is how many winners luck alone would have produced. Two wins from bets the market priced at 1.6 is noise, not an edge. Brier scores raw accuracy on every logged probability, bet or not — lower is better, 0.25 is a coin flip. It is blank for tipsters by design: naming a side states no probability, so there is nothing to calibrate. Compare on ROI, never on P/L — a tipster backs every game it calls while a model bets only where it disagrees with the price, so turnover differs by an order of magnitude. Polymarket cannot win its own table: its price is what everything else is measured against.

These are listed rather than dropped so the roster stays honest: a source missing from a board is indistinguishable from a source with nothing to say. Adding one is a single function returning {a, b, pick} or {a, b, prob_a} per contest; the matching, staking, settling and scoring are already shared. Cloudflare is no longer a blocker either — Scores24 is fetched through a real headless browser, and any other site behind the same wall can reuse that step.

Method

Every source is logged before kick-off at the price available then, staked flat $100, and settled on the real result. Tipsters name a side and are backed every time; models and books state a probability and are backed only on a 3pp disagreement with the price.

Prices and settlement. Polymarket is the venue wherever it lists a contest, and settles it. Kalshi is the venue for soccer — Polymarket lists barely any soccer matches — and for any fight, match or game Polymarket is missing. On Kalshi a tip is backed at the ask, the price backing it would actually cost, and only where that book is tight (a spread of 10¢ or less). Soccer is three-way: a Draw tip wins on a draw, and a backed side loses to it and is never refunded.

What would falsify a source. A positive ROI under 30 settled bets is not a finding. Beating the price is the only test that counts — a high hit rate on heavy favourites is not an edge, it is just backing the favourite.
