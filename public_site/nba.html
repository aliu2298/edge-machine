NBA pace labels

A team is labelled on its last 5 games — does it score more than average, does it allow more than average — and the two labels are combined into an expected total. Preseason only. No prices, no bets.

This is a test of the mechanism, not a forecast. It asks whether a label that is supposed to move transitions cleanly: every scheduled game labelled and projected before tip-off, every finished game graded, the window advancing as results land. Preseason basketball predicts nothing — starters play fifteen minutes and the bench decides the result — which is exactly why it is the right place to find the breaks.

The mechanism is holding: every labellable game carries a label, an expectation and — once played — a graded result.
Teams flipping their label so far: TOR ×1. A flip means a team crossed the scores-more or allows-more line between games. At a 5-game window this is expected to be frequent: the same seed below agreed with those teams' own full-season 2025-26 labels for only 15 of 30 teams, with Memphis 16.9 points out on defence because eliminated teams rest starters in April. A five-game window is measured, and too short — the fix is a wider window or shrinkage toward a prior, not a different five games.
