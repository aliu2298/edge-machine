How the record is kept

Learning: before every window, each trigger's score on a market is the sum of (won − price) over today's observed windows (midnight to midnight CT) where it would have fired, plus a third of the same over the previous 3 days; the best trigger is used only with at least 4 such windows today and a score above 0, otherwise the market is Off. Two losing bets in a row turn a market Off for its next 4 windows. When more than one market fires, only the highest score is bet. Observed = every window where S2 filled, bet or not.

Backtest (report only, not a gate; 2026-10-25..2026-11-09 CT, candle quotes without the print check): 906 bets +0.6% after fees, against every bet −3.2% (3942) and fixed after 2 −8.4% (516); it beat every bet on 7 of 16 days and was profitable on 7; shuffling each day's outcome order 200 times puts the real result at the 81th percentile (below 95: not significant).

Methodwhy a day is one bet · scheduling

Last scan Nov 11, 7:04 AM CT · 330 quotes · next window Nov 11, 10:00–11:30 AM CT (WTI)

A day is one bet because the commodities move in pairs: on the ledger's own graded rungs gold and silver moved together at +0.89 and WTI and Brent at +0.84, with copper riding the metals, so a day where both members of a pair fire is reported per commodity here and judged as one outcome in the record. A market-day is a trading day: a weekend or a US exchange holiday is no market-day at all, so the 30-day read takes about seven weeks. Each contract is logged at the Yes ask inside its window with the bid, ask and ask size it was taken at, settled on Kalshi's own result at a flat $100 stake, and the day is graded as a single outcome against backing every in-band rung that day.

Scheduling. Two reads a day, one per close: WTI settles at 14:30 Eastern, so its window is 10:00–11:30 Central, and the other five settle at 17:00 Eastern, so theirs is 12:30–2:00 Central, the crypto lane's window. Each is asked for from the always-on box inside its own window by the same stamp-file mechanism the crypto lane uses, because GitHub's cron cannot hold a 90-minute window. The close is matched in Eastern, not UTC, so the November clock change moves it with the exchange.

The near-certain lanes. Each contract is logged at the ask of the side it backs, and the No book is read as the Yes book's complement. A market belongs to one lane only, in the order favourite band, near-certain Yes, near-certain No. These lanes win about 3¢ a time, so one loss costs about 16 wins; they are read at 30 market-days like the others.

Reading the favourite band's figures. A day is Won when the average P&L of its rungs is above zero, and the ROI is the mean of those day P&Ls, so the two are not the same count. The record counts 16 of 22 days Won against 16.6 priced; the same bets read rung by rung are 57 of 66 won (86%) against 75% priced. The 6 lost days cost 7% to 53% of a stake each (mean 21%), not a whole one, because their winning rungs paid part of the losing ones. A lane can therefore win fewer days than the prices imply and still return a positive ROI: a lost day loses a fraction of a stake while a won day earns most of one.
