What this rule does
Pre-registered 2026-09-19 (Connors' published RSI-2 rule, thresholds unchanged). Buy the next open when a stock closes above its 200-day average, below its 5-day average, with a 2-period RSI under 5. Leave at the open after the first close back above the 5-day average, or after 10 sessions, whichever comes first. The oldest published mean-reversion rule there is, which is the point: if a rule this well known still pays after costs, the Sandbox will show it; if it does not, that is the answer for every variant of it.

What this rule does
Pre-registered 2026-09-22, Larry Connors' published rule unchanged: on SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA, when the index is above its 200-day average and closes at its lowest close of the last 7 sessions, buy the next open; leave at the open after it closes at its highest close of the last 7 (or after 20 sessions). Tests still show it working on the main indices; it lags buy-and-hold only because it sits out most days, which is why it is judged against cash, not against SPY over its own days.
Backtest before the lane went live (Jul 31 to Sep 17, not part of the record above): 76 entry days, 164 trades, +0.94% a day v cash, t +4.16.
How the record is kept
Each rule is pre-registered: its thresholds and the reason for them are fixed before it logs a trade. A trade is logged only from bars that closed BEFORE it, and enters at the next bar's open — never the signal bar's close. Costs of 5bp a side are charged on entry and exit. Judged per entry day, because names bought the same morning rise and fall together, and against SPY over the identical days: beating a rising market is not an edge. Read at 30+ entry days (Working, No edge or Proven); from 10 it is Collecting n/30 with its lean, Promising or Behind; under 10 a rule is only Too early. Gold Asia-open breakout (paper)

Definition: the first minute after 8:00 pm CT that closes beyond the 7:30–8:00 pm range (A) or the 7:55–8:00 pm range (B) on Coinbase's GOL gold future; enter at the next minute's open, exit 15 minutes later. Paper · never places an order · not in Production.

How the gold breakout is defined
One contract is 1 oz. A's range is the 30-minute candle as a chart draws it; B's needs all five minutes to have traded. The side is the first minute within 60 minutes of 8:00 pm that closes beyond the range, and it is decided only once that minute has finished; no stop. Each variant is also kept from a fixed 01:00 UTC anchor (the China open): the same time as 8:00 pm CT until Nov 1, then 7:00 pm CST. Sunday–Thursday nights.

How the record is kept
The range is the high and low of the 1-minute bars in the variant's window (A 9:30–10:00 ET, B 9:30–9:35, C 9:25–9:30 premarket). The signal is the first minute, before 11:00 ET, that closes above the high (long) or below the low (short); entry is the next minute's open and exit the close 15 or 30 minutes later. No signal by 11:00 is no break; market holidays and half-days are skipped. Gross is open to close on SIP bars, read once they are 15 minutes old, and every decision uses only bars closed by that minute. Net buys at the ask and sells at the bid from the quote taken at the start of the entry and exit minutes: IEX top of book (this data plan refuses the SIP quote), and no commission. Days counts settled plus no-break days; t is on net when quotes exist, else gross. The backtest on 807 days, done the same way, was flat to negative; this lane watches it live.
