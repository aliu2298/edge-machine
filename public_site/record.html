The lift sections carry no odds and are not profit; the one place money is measured is the priced section, which grades every lead at the Bovada line captured when it was first listed. Everywhere else each rate is compared against what the teams involved manage anyway — the named side's own rate for a claim about one team, the two sides' mean for a fixture-level outcome. A league average would credit a lead for team quality: measured here, that difference moved "team to score" from -1.5pp to +9.3pp. On-fire runs get no baseline at all — three were tried and all three were wrong, so that section is measured against a shuffled schedule instead. The reference is the number; a rate alone is unreadable, and three lanes in this repo have already died from being read without one.

Leads at the price — does it pay?

Flat 1 unit on every lead at the Bovada line captured the first build it was listed — never revised, never taken after kickoff. Three fixture-level markets are logged on every lead, fixed in advance, so no market is chosen after seeing which one paid; a side to score 2+ lead is also priced on its own claim from the book's team total. Each lane is judged on its own claim. Break-even is the hit rate the average price demands; book fair is the probability the sportsbook itself implies with the vig stripped out — a real edge has to clear both, and on the day this was added over 1.5 traded at ~1.20, a break-even of 83% against leads that hit 82%.

Model v book — is any estimate better than the price?

Every streak rule here was measured against the teams' own rates and none lifted them, because a run is the noisiest estimate of a rate there is. The number that pays is against the book. So each priced lead also carries a model probability — independent Poissons on each side's shrunk attack and defence ratings (see model.py), logged at the same moment as the price and never revised — and both are scored on the leads that have since settled. Brier is the mean squared error of a probability: lower is better, and the only question that matters is whether the model's is lower than the book's.

The book, on every fixture — who pays at the price?

Every competitive fixture in the pull is priced once it is inside 24h of kickoff — whatever the form on either side — and graded on the final score. That makes it a calibration ledger for the book itself, with no selection by streak: hits against the hits the book's own vig-free probabilities predicted. Excess is hits minus expected; z is that in standard deviations, and it is the number to read — flat-stake ROI is shown for the money view, but at n=10 its standard deviation is about 24 points.
